# `F-G5-2` — cells marked UNOCCUPIED still carry solid geometry

**Status: OPEN — root cause identified 2026-09-09, repair not yet validated.** Split from `F-G5-1` on 2026-09-09, which resolved the larger
half of the same disagreement.

## What remains after the F-G5-1 repair

`F-G5-1` established that the occupancy decode treated 54% of Hospital's cells —
every UNKNOWN one — as free space, and repaired it to `buf != UNOCCUPIED`.

That repair does not account for all of the disagreement. In the transect
through route 018's closest-approach point, cells the generator marked
**explicitly UNOCCUPIED (value 5)** still return PhysX-solid geometry:

```
            route point ↓
map   (x): ????????????????##.................................
physx (x): --S--------SSSSSSSSSSSSSSS-------------------------
```

The OCCUPIED sliver ends at about −0.8 m; PhysX reports solid out to the route
point at 0.0 m. Roughly 0.8 m of wall is positively mislabelled as empty, not
merely unclassified, so `buf != UNOCCUPIED` cannot recover it.

Vertical scans confirm the geometry is present through the whole slab
(0.02 m to 0.59 m at 0.0005 m), so this is not the band mismatch either.

## Why it is not yet diagnosed

Ruled out by measurement in `F-G5-1`: units, footprint inflation, grid axis
order, and the slab lower bound. What remains is inside the generator — its
sampling method, its treatment of instanced geometry, or a sub-cell transform
difference between the generator's traversal and PhysX scene queries. That needs
generator-level investigation rather than another consumer-side check.

## The procedural consequence, which does not wait for the diagnosis

**G5 must remain PhysX-backed and must never be re-derived from the occupancy
grid.** The map is the artefact under suspicion; asking it to certify routes it
planned is the circularity G5 exists to break, and it is now known to be wrong in
a direction that would pass unsafe routes.

The map-derived clearance stays recorded beside the measurement, labelled as not
the gate.

## What must not be concluded

- Not that the map is unusable. With UNKNOWN blocked it is far more conservative
  than before, and it remains the planner's input.
- Not that PhysX is authoritative in general — only that where the two disagree
  here, PhysX matched the geometry and the map did not.
- Not that a route passing G5 is contact-safe. F-TWIN-1 remains open, and G5
  supports route-clearance acceptance only.

# ROOT CAUSE — the occupancy generator omits solid geometry

Measured on Hospital by sampling each object's world-space footprint against the
map it produced:

| object | z-span | footprint cells | marked occupied |
|---|---|---|---|
| `SM_ReceptionDesk_01a2_52` | 0.0 – 1.321 | 725 | **0 (0.0%)** |
| `SM_SupplyCart_03a_126` | 0.0 – 1.034 | 72 | **0 (0.0%)** |
| `Geo_W_WallBase10` | 0.0 – 3.0 | 80 | 10 (12.5%) |
| `Geo_M1_TrimInsideSide` | 0.001 – 0.159 | 120 | 16 (13.3%) |
| `SM_Chart_Rack_01d_37` | 0.001 – 1.638 | 65 | 38 (58.5%) |

A reception desk and a supply cart, both over a metre tall and squarely inside
the slab z ∈ [0.10, 0.60], are **entirely absent** from the map. A wall spanning
the full 3 m height is present at 12.5% of its footprint.

This is not the `UNKNOWN` decode of `F-G5-1` — these cells are positively marked
`UNOCCUPIED`. It is a separate failure of the generator to represent geometry
that PhysX reports without difficulty.

It explains the corrected-map baseline exactly: 10 candidates now satisfy the
full long-horizon requirement and all 10 fail G5 at 0.00007–0.0045 m, striking
precisely this class of object — trim, wall bases, a supply cart, a chart rack,
a reception desk.

## Attempted repair — PhysX-built occupancy, NOT VALIDATED

`Collector.build_occupancy_physx` builds the grid from PhysX queries instead, so
the map and G5 share one instrument and a route cannot be planned through
geometry the gate will then find. It is **inert**: defined, called by nothing,
and marked `EXPERIMENTAL, NOT VALIDATED` in its own docstring.

Two things were learned building it, and both are why it is not finished.

**Raycast is the wrong primitive.** The first version cast a ray downward from
the top of the slab. A ray starting inside a collider reports no hit, so every
object taller than the slab — every wall — vanished. Caught by checking a known
wall: `Geo_W_WallBase10` went from 12.5% to **0%**. Validating against an object
whose answer was already known is the only reason this was noticed.

**Hospital's walls are hollow shell meshes.** `Geo_W_WallBase10` spans
y 1.65–1.75 and its collider is the two faces, not a solid. At the wall centre
`overlap_sphere(0.05)` finds nothing and `overlap_sphere(0.20)` finds two hits.
So a cell-centred volumetric probe can sit in the void *between a wall's own
faces* and report free space. Furniture behaves correctly — the supply cart
returns a hit at its centre — so the defect is specific to thin shell geometry,
which is exactly what walls are.

A probe that cannot fall between faces is needed: an overlap box inflated past
the cell boundary, or a swept test. It must be validated against known walls
**and** known furniture together, because the raycast attempt fixed furniture
while silently destroying walls.

## Status of the programme while this is open

- **G5 stays PhysX-backed** and is never re-derived from the occupancy grid.
- **Production collection is BLOCKED.** Every long-horizon-adequate route in
  Hospital collides, because the planner is blind to obstacles the gate can see.
- The frozen Hospital and Office route-set results are unaffected: they record
  what the pipeline produced, and both remain `NO_ADMISSIBLE_ROUTE`.
