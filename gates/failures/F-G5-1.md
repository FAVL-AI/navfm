# `F-G5-1` — the PhysX clearance instrument and the planner's map disagree by two orders of magnitude

**Status: RESOLVED 2026-09-09.** Root cause below. Raised 2026-09-09 from the Hospital route-admission artefact
`gates/scenes/hospital_route_admission_20260907.json`.

## What was observed

Seven of 96 Hospital candidates reached G5 (they are exactly the seven that
satisfy the frozen long-horizon requirement). All seven failed it. For each, the
two clearance readings recorded side by side disagree sharply:

| route | PhysX min clearance | map-derived, on the inflated grid | probe height of the closest hit | closest prim |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 006 | 0.0003 m | 0.1 m | 0.12 m | `Geo_W_TrimStrateBase5/…` |
| 018 | 0.0005 m | 0.1 m | 0.05 m | `Geo_M2_BaseWallSide3/…` |
| 021 | 0.0008 m | 0.1 m | 0.05 m | `Geo_M2_TrimStrateINside13_232/…` |
| 040 | 0.0009 m | 0.1 m | 0.05 m | `Geo_W_WallBase6/…` |
| 066 | 0.0141 m | 0.1 m | 0.05 m | `Geo_M3_DoorWall3/…` |
| 076 | 0.0203 m | 0.0 m | 0.05 m | `SM_WheelChair_01a3_123/…` |
| 092 | 0.0082 m | 0.1 m | 0.05 m | `Geo_W_TrimStrateBase6/…` |

The map-derived column is a distance transform **on the grid already inflated by
`footprint_radius` = 0.24 m**, so 0.1 m there means roughly 0.34 m from the face
of a *mapped* obstacle. PhysX reports sub-millimetre to 2 cm. The units are not
the explanation: `OccupancyGrid.clearance()` returns cells and the harness
multiplies by `grid_resolution`, which is correct.

## Why it is not obviously an instrument fault

Six of the seven closest hits are at the **0.05 m** probe height, and the prims
are wall-base trim and skirting — `Geo_W_TrimStrateBase`, `Geo_M2_BaseWallSide`,
`Geo_W_WallBase`, `Geo_M3_DoorWall`. That is exactly the geometry class the
frozen route-admission contract predicted would be invisible to the planner: the
occupancy slab is absolute z ∈ [0.10, 0.60], so anything topping out below
0.10 m is unmapped by construction.

And it is a **real** hazard rather than a conservative one. The simulated robot's
collision model (`gates/scenes/hospital_g2_obstacle_map_integrity_20260907.json`)
puts the wheel colliders at z ∈ [≈0, 0.080] and the non-wheel body at
[0.0768, 0.2803]. Geometry at 0.05 m is inside the wheel band. Skirting at that
height would strike the wheels, not pass under the chassis.

So the reading is consistent with the contract's own prediction, and the
direction of the discrepancy (PhysX stricter than the map) is the direction the
gate exists to catch.

## Why it is nevertheless unresolved

Consistency with a prediction is not the same as verification, and one thing
does not fit: 0.34 m of disagreement is far more than wall skirting protrudes.
Skirting stands proud of a wall face by centimetres. Two candidate explanations
remain open and are not distinguished by the present evidence:

1. **Unmapped geometry.** Real obstruction in the blind band that the map cannot
   see, protruding much further than trim alone would — the wheelchair on route
   076 is that shape of object, though it spans well above 0.10 m and should be
   mapped.
2. **A grid/world coordinate offset.** If `origin` or `world_to_cell` is
   misaligned, the map-derived reading is meaningless *and* route positions are
   wrong relative to real geometry. The passing support results do not rule this
   out: a floor exists nearly everywhere, so support probes pass under an offset
   too.

## Why this matters, and to what

`gates/BO10/hospital_bo10_frozen_routeset_20260909.json` freezes Hospital as
`NO_ADMISSIBLE_ROUTE`. That verdict rests on two independent legs:

- **89 of 96 fail G10.** Pure route geometry, recomputable from the recorded
  polylines with no simulator. Unaffected by this finding.
- **7 of 96 fail G5.** Rests entirely on the instrument this finding questions.

If the PhysX clearance instrument is wrong, those seven require re-adjudication
and Hospital would not necessarily be `NO_ADMISSIBLE_ROUTE`. The freeze records
this dependency rather than presenting both legs as equally settled.

## What must not be concluded

- Not that G5 is broken. The reading may well be correct, and the physical
  reasoning above is in its favour.
- Not that the seven routes are safe. A gate disagreeing with a map is not
  licence to prefer the map — particularly the map that planned the route, which
  is the circularity G5 exists to break.
- Not that Hospital is unsuitable. The claim boundary in the freeze artefact
  stands unchanged.

## Resolution requires

A targeted Isaac measurement, not reasoning: pick one failing route, place the
robot at the reported closest-approach sample, and compare the PhysX overlap
against the occupancy cell that the same world coordinate maps to. That
distinguishes the two hypotheses directly. It is **not** a prerequisite for
Office, which is measured under the same instrument and would surface the same
discrepancy if it is systematic — and if Office shows ordinary clearance values
on ordinary routes, that is itself evidence about which explanation holds.

# RESOLUTION — the planner's obstacle map under-reports occupied space

Resolved by targeted measurement on Hospital, as this finding required. The
PhysX instrument was **not** wrong. The map was.

## Measurement 1 — the map's own value histogram

The Isaac occupancy generator writes three values. Hospital, 0.10 m, slab
z ∈ [0.10, 0.60]:

| value | meaning | cells | share |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | OCCUPIED | 29,861 | 9.3% |
| 5 | UNOCCUPIED | 118,323 | 36.7% |
| 6 | **UNKNOWN** | **174,376** | **54.1%** |

`build_occupancy` decoded this as `grid = buf == 4`. Every UNKNOWN cell —
the majority of the map — became free space.

After inflation by the footprint radius:

| decode rule | free cells | share of grid |
|---|---|---|
| `buf == OCCUPIED` (old) | 242,106 | 75.1% |
| `buf != UNOCCUPIED` (new) | 91,650 | 28.4% |

**62% of everywhere the planner believed it could drive was cells the generator
had declined to classify.** This is the FL-019 genus at map scale: an absence of
knowledge consumed as a benign value. The direction is the dangerous one — a
missing obstacle never invents a wall, it invents floor exactly where the robot
would collide.

## Measurement 2 — a transect through a failing wall

Through route 018's closest-approach point, ±2.5 m, 0.10 m steps. `#` OCCUPIED,
`.` UNOCCUPIED, `?` UNKNOWN; `S` PhysX solid within 6 cm:

```
            route point ↓
map   (x): ????????????????##.................................
physx (x): --S--------SSSSSSSSSSSSSSS-------------------------

map   (y): ##.................................................
physx (y): SSSSSSSSSSSSSSSSSSSSSSSSSSSS-----------------------
```

The wall's interior is UNKNOWN, only a two-cell surface sliver is OCCUPIED, and
the route point sits where the map says UNOCCUPIED and PhysX says solid.

## Measurement 3 — the slab was not the explanation

Vertical scans at each failing point, PhysX minimum horizontal distance by
height:

```
route_018   0.02:0.0005  0.10:0.0005  0.30:0.0005  0.59:0.0005
route_040   0.02:0.0009  0.10:0.0009  0.30:0.0009  0.59:0.0009
```

Geometry is present at every height **inside** the slab, so the band-mismatch
hypothesis in the original finding — geometry hiding below z = 0.10 — is
**refuted** as the cause. It remains a real hazard class; it is not what
happened here.

## Answers to the questions this finding was opened with

- **units** — correct. `OccupancyGrid.clearance()` returns cells; the harness
  multiplies by `grid_resolution`.
- **footprint inflation** — correct and conservative. `inflate` uses
  `ceil(0.24 / 0.10) = 3` cells = 0.30 m, exceeding the 0.24 m radius.
- **grid discretisation / axes** — correct. Generator `dims = (768, 420)`,
  reshaped `(dims[1], dims[0]) = (420, 768) = (ny, nx)`, and `world_to_cell`
  maps x→col, y→row. Consistent.
- **signed/unsigned distance** — not implicated; both readings are unsigned
  distances to geometry.
- **centre-vs-surface** — not the cause at this magnitude.
- **planner vs PhysX clearance** — the disagreement is real and the map is the
  party in error.

## Repair

`grid = buf != UNOCCUPIED`. Blocked is "not explicitly unoccupied", never "not
explicitly occupied", so an unclassified cell and an unexpected value are both
blocked. `tests/test_occupancy_decode.py` holds it, including a connectivity
control in which a wall with an unknown interior and a gapped surface is
traversable under the old rule and sealed under the new one.

## A residual defect remains OPEN

The repair does not close everything. In the transect, cells explicitly marked
**UNOCCUPIED** still carry PhysX-solid geometry — roughly 0.8 m of wall beyond
the OCCUPIED sliver. `buf != UNOCCUPIED` cannot fix that, because those cells
are positively mislabelled rather than unclassified.

So the map remains untrustworthy in a second, narrower way, and the consequence
is procedural: **G5 must stay PhysX-backed and must never be re-derived from the
occupancy grid.** The gate that caught this is the only instrument that could
have. Tracked as **F-G5-2**.

## Consequence for the frozen results — and a retraction

Hospital's 7 G5 failures are **genuine**: those routes really do run into
geometry, so `HOSPITAL_BO10_FROZEN_ROUTESET = NO_ADMISSIBLE_ROUTE` is
unaffected and its weaker leg is now the stronger one.

But the *interpretation* recorded in
`gates/BO10/office_bo10_frozen_routeset_20260909.json` must be retracted in
part. It attributed the straight-route problem to `smooth_path` string-pulling.
That mechanism is real, but it is not established as the dominant cause: with
75% of the grid decoded as free, most of the scene looked like open space, and a
shortest-path planner in open space produces straight lines whatever its
smoothing does.

The **measurements** stand — 73/96 and 84/96 zero-turn candidates are what the
pipeline produced. The **causal claim** does not, and is downgraded to:

> Under a map that decoded 62% of its free space from unclassified cells, the
> generator produced predominantly straight routes. How much of that survives a
> corrected map is **UNMEASURED**.

That question is now answerable and must be answered before a turning-route
population is designed against the wrong cause.
